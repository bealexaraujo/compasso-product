# Users

## Primary Persona

**Marina Souza**
Clinical psychologist, 28 years old, São Paulo/SP.
Autonomous professional operating as Pessoa Física. 2 years into private practice.

Marina graduated from a 5-year psychology program that covered clinical theory,
research methods, and ethics in depth. Fiscal compliance was never mentioned once.

On her first week working independently, a colleague told her she needed to
register with the CRP and pay INSS. That was the full onboarding she received
for running her own finances.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Professional status** | Autonomous PF, no CNPJ |

| **Monthly income** | R$ 1.500 – R$ 3.000 (early career) |

---

### Goals

- Keep her practice legally compliant without spending mental energy on it
- Focus on her patients, not on bureaucracy
- Avoid fines and surprises at year-end tax filing (DIRPF)
- Understand what she actually owes, in plain language

---

### Frustrations

- **"I don't even know what I don't know."** Marina is aware she has
  obligations, but has never seen a complete, reliable list of all of them.
  She learns about new requirements when she hears from a colleague who
  already missed one.

- **Every portal is a different fight.** e-CAC, Meu INSS, CRP-SP, the
  Prefeitura... Each has its own login, its own interface, and its own way
  of confirming that something was done.

- **The monthly cycle is exhausting.** At the end of every month, Marina
  goes through a mental checklist from memory or a simple calendar event.
  She's not confident the list is complete.

- **Receipts are everywhere.** PDFs in email. Screenshots on her phone.
  A GPS boleto she may or may not have paid. When tax season arrives, she
  spends hours reconstructing what she did.

- **She can't afford to get it wrong.** A late INSS payment affects her
  future retirement contributions. A missed Carnê-Leão generates fines
  and interest. The stakes are real, even if the amounts are small.

## What Marina Is Not

Understanding who Compasso is **not** for is as important as defining
who it is for.

**Marina is not a business owner.** She has no CNPJ, no employees, no
invoicing system. She is a sole professional who earns from her sessions.
Products built for companies (accounting SaaS, ERP tools) add complexity,
not clarity.

**Marina is not a tax expert who needs automation.** She needs to learn
what her obligations are before she can manage them. A pure task manager
solves the wrong job.

**Marina is not interested in financial planning right now.** Investments,
savings goals, and budgeting apps address a different life stage. She needs
compliance first.

---

## User Segments Within the Persona

All users in the MVP share Marina's profile, but differ along two dimensions
that affect which obligations apply:

| Segment | Key variable | Obligation impact |
|---|---|---|
| **Receita Saúde active** | Logs sessions on the federal platform | Weekly + monthly Receita Saúde obligations |
| **CCM registered** | Has municipal registration (SP) | Annual CCM renewal required |
| **High session volume** | 20+ sessions/month | Higher Carnê-Leão base, mandatory registration |
| **Convênio / insurance** | Works with health insurance plans | Additional documentation obligations |

The onboarding flow maps each user to their specific obligation profile
based on answers to these dimensions.

## What This Means for the Product

Marina's profile shapes every product decision:

- **Language must be non-technical.** She doesn't know what "competência" 
  means in fiscal context. Plain Portuguese, no jargon.

- **Education before task management.** The product needs to explain what
  each obligation is and why it exists, not just remind her to do it.

- **Proactive, not reactive.** Marina won't open the app to check. The
  product needs to come to her, at the right time, with the right
  information.

- **Proof of completion matters more than check-off.** "I think I did it"
  is not a reliable state. The system should confirm, not trust.

- **Affordable by design.** Early-career psychologists have constrained
  margins. The product must be priced for accessibility, not as a
  professional service.
